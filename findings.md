
# Findings and Recommendations

## 1. Executive Summary

This project examines 12 selected relationships between NIST CSF 2.0 subcategories and CIS Controls v8.1 safeguards.
The analysis illustrates how practical cybersecurity safeguards can support broader cybersecurity outcomes and highlights areas where complementary measures may be necessary.

## 4. Key Findings

### 4.1 Governance Requires Organizational Oversight
The selected governance safeguards support incident accountability and supplier assessment.
However, broader governance objectives require additional organizational policies, clearly defined responsibilities, management oversight and continuous risk monitoring.

### 4.2 Strong Alignment in Foundational Security Practices
The selected asset management and security awareness safeguards demonstrate strong alignment with their corresponding NIST outcomes.
These practices contribute to establishing foundational cybersecurity capabilities.

### 4.3 Multiple Safeguards May Be Necessary
A single safeguard may not fully address a broader cybersecurity outcome.
For example, role-based access control supports appropriate authorization, but access granting, revocation and periodic review processes are also necessary.

### 4.4 Documentation Does Not Guarantee Effectiveness
The existence of security policies, procedures and technical configurations does not automatically demonstrate effective implementation.
Organizations should validate controls through testing, monitoring and evidence review.

### 4.5 Recovery Requires More Than Backups
Backup testing contributes to organizational resilience.
However, effective recovery also requires integrity verification, recovery priorities, documented responsibilities and coordination with relevant stakeholders.

## 5. Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| High | Establish clearly defined cybersecurity responsibilities. |
| High | Maintain an accurate inventory of organisational assets. |
| High | Implement comprehensive access management processes. |
| High | Establish recurring vulnerability assessment procedures. |
| High | Develop and test incident response and recovery plans. |
| Medium | Conduct recurring supplier cybersecurity assessments. |
| Medium | Evaluate the effectiveness of security awareness training. |
| Medium | Maintain traceable evidence of control implementation. |
These priorities are illustrative and would need to be adjusted according to an organization's risk profile.

## 6. Conclusion

NIST CSF 2.0 and CIS Controls v8.1 can complement one another in cybersecurity risk management.
NIST CSF provides a structure for defining cybersecurity outcomes, while CIS Controls offer practical measures that can contribute to achieving those outcomes.
Combining outcome-based frameworks with actionable safeguards can support cybersecurity governance, risk management and control implementation.
However, organizations should avoid assuming that the implementation of individual safeguards automatically demonstrates comprehensive framework alignment or regulatory compliance.
